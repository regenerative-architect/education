# License notes

These notes explain the license in plain words and flag a few questions for the project's steward. They aren't legal advice.

## The license

The toolkit is licensed under the **GNU Affero General Public License, version 3 or later** (AGPL-3.0-or-later), as stated in the education repository's `LICENSE` file. The full text is in [`LICENSE`](LICENSE).

## In plain words

**You can**

- use it in classrooms, schools, homes and communities;
- adapt, remix and translate it;
- share it with anyone.

**You're asked to**

- keep adaptations open under the same license;
- credit the Planetary Restoration Archive;
- share improvements back with the community.

The project also states ethical-use intentions: the toolkit isn't meant for organizations that harm the environment, violate human rights, discriminate, or close off educational resources for profit. If you see a problem, start with a respectful conversation. Most license issues are misunderstandings that can be fixed together, and repair comes before any formal step.

## Notes for the steward

1. **Added terms and the AGPL.** Section 7 of the AGPL treats extra restrictions added to an AGPL-licensed work as "further restrictions" that anyone who receives it may remove. Ethical-use terms attached to AGPL code may therefore not work as license conditions, though they still state the community's intent clearly. If enforceable ethical-use conditions matter, a license written for that purpose would be needed, and that's worth discussing with a lawyer.
2. **One license per kind of work.** Some documents in the education repository say they're under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0, while its `LICENSE` file says AGPL-3.0. Choosing one license for lessons and guides (CC BY-SA 4.0 is common for teaching materials) and one for code (AGPL-3.0) would remove the ambiguity for teachers who want to reuse them.
3. **What this repository carries.** The education repository's `LICENSE` file adds enforcement language (monitoring, penalties and reporting). This app repository doesn't copy that language; it keeps the intent through the plain-words summary above and the repair-first approach of the Zero Harm and Anti-Coercion note. The original terms remain in the education repository, and the steward can decide whether to bring them here.
